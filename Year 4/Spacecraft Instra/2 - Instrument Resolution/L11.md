What are imaging vs non-imaging instruments?

Back: 
- Imaging – a system that measures the intensity of radiation as a function of position on the Earth’s surface (or object being observed) so that a 2D image of radiation intensity can be generated (e.g. cameras, scanners)
- Non-imaging – either does not measure the intensity of radiation or does not do so as a function of position on the Earth’s surface (or object being observed)

What does it mean for an instrument to have selectable band widths? Why would this be useful?

Back: 
- It means you can subtly adjust the band being looked at
- Infrared sensors are especially susceptible to spectral drift, this lets you compensate for it
- The exact spectra we want to observe can be highly specific to the thing we're actually trying to measure, so if we can adjust the target bands during the mission we have much more flexibility

What does a diffraction grating do? What is it?

Back: 
- a collection of equally spaced transmitting or reflecting elements separated by a distance comparable to the wavelength of light.
- Diffracts light, changing it's reflected angle slightly based on frequency (alternative to prism)
- Diffraction Gratings use diffraction to disperse light
- Angular separation of interference peaks is wavelength-dependent
