What are the properties of PV InSb?

Back: 
- Indium antimonide photodiode detectors are photovoltaic generating electric current when subjected to infrared radiation.
- InSb has high quantum efficiency (80-90%). (Strong response to incident radiation, HIGH sensativity -> lots of use in space)
- Its drawback is a high instability over time; the detector characteristics tend to drift over time, and between cooldowns
- requiring periodical recalibrations, increasing the complexity of the imaging system
- InSb detectors also require cooling, as they have to operate at cryogenic temperatures (typically 80 K).
- For IR applications

What is the application and basic working principle PC CMT?

Back: 
- Cadmium mercury teluride photoconductive detectors. Photons with energy greater than the semiconductor band-gap energy excite electrons into the conduction band, thereby increasing the conductivity of the material. 
- They are for IR applications

START_CARD
Basic

State important metrics when selecting IR sensors

Back: 
- Responsivity: electrical output for a given IR input
- Quantum Efficiency: number of output electrons for every single incident photon
- Spectral response: How the responsivity varies with the wavelength of the IR power
- Linearity: How well the output signal “tracks” the IR power 
- Dynamic range: The range of IR signal levels for which the detector is useful (lower and upper limits for the "linearity" point)
- Crosstalk: Apparent signal from one detector due to a large signal on a nearby detector (basically blooming)
- Modulation transfer function (MTF): How the responsivity varies as smaller and smaller targets are focused on the detector

END_CARD

Explain Specific detectivity.

Back: 
- We often classify and select infrared detectors according to their spectral band of operation and a figure of merit called specific detectivity or quantum efficiency for photon detectors.
- This is basically the amount of signal that can be detected per unit size. 
- It's a really important metric for IR detector selection

Basic

When selecting an IR detector, what are the driving factors?

Back: 
We want to:
- achieve maximum detectivity (or sufficient as a compramise)
- minimise cooling requirements
- have a sufficiently responsive detector (small time constant)
- minimise cost
- minimise geometry constraints
- cover the required wavelength bands
- NOTE: longer wavelengths become especially problematic when it comes to cooling
