Why are CMOS and CCD sensors silicon? Describe their mechanism.

Back: 
- Because they use the photo-electric effect
- When a photon of an appropriate wavelength (in general between 200 and 1000 nm) hits silicon, it generates an electron-hole pair
- If an electric field is present, the electron and the hole are separated and charge can accumulate, proportional to the number of incident photons
- therefore the scene imaged onto the detector will be reproduced if a proper X-Y structure is present

Explain the working principle of CCD.

Back: 
- The electronic shutter is opened allowing light to hit the silicon
- The incident light hits a silicon surface, which causes charge to accumulate where it hits due to the photoelectric effect
- Aluminium strips prevent charge from moving off its pixel
- Once the exposures complete we close the electronic shutter to block light
- We can shift the accumulated electrons down by one pixel, shifting them along the silicon. The lowest row will reach a converter
- Repeat with each row until the silicons been processed, now you have image data
- Open the shutter and repeat for next image
