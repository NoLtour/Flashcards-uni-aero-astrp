What is Bragg Scattering from Water Surfaces, how can scatteromiters use this to get wind direction/speed?

Back: 
- Bragg Scattering is a phenomenon where radar waves reflect strongly from ocean surface waves whose wavelengths are exactly half that of the radar signal. This resonance amplifies the radar backscatter. Scatterometers send out radar pulses at multiple angles; the strength and variation of the backscattered signal depend on the surface roughness, which is shaped by wind speed and direction. By analyzing the returned signals from different directions, scatterometers can accurately determine wind vectors over large ocean areas.

How does pulse length effect SAR resolution, and what are the trade offs for decreasing it? What resolution does it effect?

Back: 
![[Pasted image 20250514171151.png|600]]
![[Pasted image 20250514171208.png|600]]
- This is a factor in (along track) RANGE resolution
- Pulses need to be far enough apart from eachother to prevent the return signal "merging" into a single indistinguishable response
- Hence we can increase resolution by decreasing pulse length (which increases bandwitdth)
- This puts more strain on processing
- It gives less time to send/receive a pulse

	- Clearly increases energy requirements
